Two rules beat any table. First, check summit wind before you leave: above roughly 50–60 km/h gusts, the alpine lifts at Happo-one, Goryu and Tsugaike start to close. Second, on powder days the crowds follow the snow north, so your real choice is often between deeper snow with lines and slightly less snow with none.

Linked Pairs: Two Resorts, One Day

Two pairs of resorts are joined by lifts and runs, so you can ski both without getting on a bus.

Goryu – Hakuba47. The two connect near the top, between Goryu’s Alps-daira and the upper part of Hakuba47. Together they give you the most varied single day in the valley: Goryu’s long groomers and night skiing, and Hakuba47’s trees and terrain park.

  • Start at Goryu’s Escal Plaza if you need rentals or lessons. Start at the Hakuba47 base if you want the trees first.
  • The link is at altitude, so it closes when the upper lifts go on wind hold. On a windy day, plan to ski one side only.
  • Check the time of the last lift back to your side. Missing it means a shuttle or taxi ride around to your base.

Norikura – Cortina. These neighbours at the north end are linked by lifts and share a pass area. Ride the Cortina side for steep trees on a powder morning, then cross to Norikura when the lines build.

  • Both are about 40 minutes by car from Happo, so commit to the pair for the whole day.
  • Northern shuttle buses fill early on powder days. Book a ride or leave on the first bus.

A tip for both pairs: agree on a meeting point and time with your group before you split up. Phone signal is patchy on parts of the mountains, and a missed meeting can cost an hour.
