Tokyo, Narita and Haneda to Hakuba

The fastest public route from Tokyo is the bullet train to Nagano plus an express bus, about three hours. If you are landing with a lot of gear and want no transfers, a direct airport bus is the easiest option, though it takes twice as long.

Shinkansen + bus. Take the Hokuriku Shinkansen (Kagayaki, Hakutaka or Asama) from Tokyo or Ueno to Nagano. Leave by the East Exit to the Alpico bus stop for Hakuba. The bus calls at several stops in the valley, including Goryu, Hakuba Station, Happo and Tsugaike, so choose the stop closest to your lodging. This season the Nagano–Hakuba bus runs two kinds of service, reservation-only and non-reserved, with separate timetables. Book ahead in peak weeks, and check which kind your ticket is for.

Direct airport buses. Several operators run winter buses from Narita and Haneda straight to the valley, with stops at the main resort areas. They take longer than the train, but you load your bags once and sleep. Book early: they sell out around Christmas, New Year and Chinese New Year. The app’s transfer link shows current options.

Direct trains from Shinjuku. The regular Limited Express Azusa 5 leaves Shinjuku at 08:00 and reaches Hakuba at 11:40. This winter JR East adds a second direct train, the “Hakuba Express”. It leaves Shinjuku at 10:02 and arrives at 14:10, running almost daily from 19 December 2026 to 28 February 2027 (not 2–3 January). It is all reserved seats, and it runs to Hakuba only, not back. Other trains need a change at Matsumoto to the local Oito Line.

Highway bus from Shinjuku. Alpico runs buses from Busta Shinjuku to Hakuba in about five hours. Fares change by day of the week and season, and they are usually the cheapest way in. Luggage space is limited, so check the rules for ski bags.

Coming from Kansai or Hokuriku? Take the Hokuriku Shinkansen to Itoigawa. From 1 December 2026 to 31 March 2027, a temporary bus backed by JR West runs three round trips a day between Itoigawa and Hakuba, filling the gap in the Oito Line.
