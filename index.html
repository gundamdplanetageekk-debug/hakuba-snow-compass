      Hakuba ski FAQ

      Which Hakuba resort is best on a windy day?

      Lower, tree-lined resorts usually keep running when the big alpine lifts stop. Hakuba47, Iwatake, Norikura and Cortina are the usual windy-day picks. Upper lifts at Happo-one, Goryu and Tsugaike are the first to go on wind hold.

      Where does Hakuba get the most snow?

      The north end of the valley. Cortina, Norikura and Tsugaike often get noticeably more snow than the central resorts, so they are the busiest on powder days. The south end, Kashimayari and Jiigatake, gets less snow but it is often drier.

      Can I ski the backcountry from Hakuba resorts?

      Only through designated backcountry gates. Hakuba Valley lists gates at Able Hakuba Goryu, Happo-one, Tsugaike, Hakuba Norikura and Hakuba Cortina. Leaving the boundary anywhere else is not allowed. Read the Japan Avalanche Network bulletin, carry a beacon, shovel and probe, and submit a mountain itinerary before you go.

      How do I get between Hakuba resorts without a car?

      The Hakuba Valley Shuttle links all 10 resorts and is free on the day you ski with a Hakuba Valley pass. Resorts also run their own free shuttles, and the Gurutto Bus connects Omachi with Hakuba. Buses fill up on powder days, so leave early.

      How is the daily ranking calculated?

      Every hour we fetch forecasts for the summit and base of each resort, then score fresh snow, snow quality, wind and likely lift holds, visibility and live lift status against each resort's terrain profile. It is an automated estimate, so always check official lift status before you travel.
